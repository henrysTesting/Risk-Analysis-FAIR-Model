# I. About This Document

## Project Description

Within this report we will delve into various cybersecurity-related risks that Accel Entertainment could potentially encounter. Accel Entertainment is the largest terminal operator in the nation, providing slot machines and related gambling devices to well-known casinos in the mid-west, as well as proprietary software that manages those systems. Given the sensitive nature of financial transactions and personal data involved in gaming operations, Accel Entertainment is a prominent target for cyber threats.

This assessment aims to unmask key vulnerabilities within their systems — both physical and virtual — evaluate the potential impact of different threat scenarios, and recommend mitigative strategies to enhance the organization's overall cybersecurity posture.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **CVE** | Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures — a publicly accessible database cataloging known security vulnerabilities in software |
| **Competitor/Rivaling Companies** | Companies in direct competition with another |
| **Credential Stuffing** | An automated cyberattack that inserts stolen usernames and passwords |
| **Cybercriminal Groups** | Organized groups of hackers that exploit companies, usually for monetary gain |
| **Form 10-K** | An annual report U.S. public companies must file, disclosing financial conditions and operations |
| **Hacktivists** | Individuals who gain unauthorized access to systems with a politically motivated agenda |
| **Insider Threats** | Individuals within a company who pose as contributing members but harbor ulterior motives |
| **Phishing** | A social attack that attempts to steal money, identity, or personal information |
| **Script Kiddies** | Individuals with limited cybersecurity knowledge who use pre-built malicious tools |
| **Shodan** | A platform providing access to web-facing applications such as email servers and web servers hosted by companies |
| **Social Engineering** | A range of malicious activities whose primary threat vector is human interaction |

## Limitations

This report is based upon publicly available information and generalized cybersecurity practices, as direct access to Accel Entertainment's internal systems, proprietary software, and infrastructure was not feasible. Certain assumptions have been made regarding the architecture and operational procedures of their gaming and management systems. This report attempts to avoid extremes and curates scenarios that are believable based on past events from comparable companies. The dynamic nature of cybersecurity threats means that new risks may emerge beyond the timeframe of this analysis.

---

## Analytic Confidence

Analytic confidence is **moderate**. This evaluation is based on publicly available information — Accel Entertainment's 10-K filings, reported breach statistics from cybersecurity industry sources, and FAIR-based modeling techniques to estimate risk values, loss magnitudes, and frequencies. Due to limited transparency into AE's internal cybersecurity architecture and controls, certain assumptions were necessary. While estimates are grounded in industry trends and known vulnerabilities, the absence of insider information means the full scope of potential risk cannot be completely mapped.
